Sketch constraints

MechPilot keeps the shape of a sketch with constraints (horizontal, tangent, a distance of 100 mm...) and driving dimensions (a dimension whose value sets the geometry). A solver moves the geometry until every constraint holds. This page says what the solver does, what takes part, and where its limits are.

The solver

The solver is planegcs, the 2D constraint solver of FreeCAD compiled to WebAssembly (LGPL-2.0-or-later). It runs in its own worker (app/workers/solver), so the drawing never freezes while it works, and it is a separate, replaceable module: the app talks to it only through the worker protocol (shared/sketch/protocol.ts). Its licence notice is in app/workers/solver/NOTICE.md.

The code has three layers:

  • shared/sketch/model.ts turns the drawing into the solver's input (points, lines, circles, arcs and constraints as plain JSON) and the solver's output back into entities. It has no WebAssembly and is tested on its own.
  • shared/sketch/solver.ts loads planegcs, solves, reads the diagnosis and computes the degree-of- freedom colouring.
  • app/workers/solver/index.ts serves solve and freedom to the renderer.

Every solve works on the whole document and returns only the entities that changed, with their ids unchanged; the UI applies them with one updateEntities command, so a solve is undone like any other edit (product rule 4). Values are copied from the solver as they are: nothing is rounded (product rule 1). A solved value within 1e-12 mm (or rad) of the stored one keeps the stored value: that is floating-point noise (an arc end point at sin(π) = 1.2e-16 can pull a joined line end by 2e-15 mm), far below the solver's own convergence threshold, and not an edit.

Every point of a solver entity is free unless a constraint holds it: a line with only a horizontal constraint can still move up and down, left and right, and change length.

Points are named in constraints by entity and role: a/b for a line, center for a circle, center/start/end for an arc, the vertex index for a polyline. In the solver they become <entity id>.a, <entity id>.center, <entity id>.3...; constraints keep their MechPilot ids, so a conflict reported by planegcs reads back directly as the constraint the user made.

Fixed is written as one equation per degree of freedom (4 for a line, 3 for a circle, 5 for an arc), not as planegcs "fixed points": planegcs silently ignores a constraint whose unknowns are all fixed, so a horizontal constraint on a fixed sloping line would "solve" with no error. With equations, such a contradiction is reported like any other. A fixed entity is never moved by a solve, even when the sketch is over-constrained.

Tangent at a shared end point (a fillet corner: a line or arc ending where an arc starts) is written the way FreeCAD's Sketcher does it: angle_via_point through the shared point, with the angle between the two curves' normals there set to 0 or π (whichever the current shape is closer to). The distance-based tangent_la / tangent_aa is degenerate when the tangency point is also the joined end point: planegcs reports it as redundant and it holds nothing. The end points count as shared when a coincident constraint joins an end of one to an end of the other, or when they lie within 1e-9 mm of each other; in the second case a coincident is added with the tangent.

Two pairs are checked before solving, because planegcs accepts them by shrinking a line to a point: horizontal and vertical on the same line, and parallel and perpendicular on the same two lines. They are reported as a conflict; the later constraint of the pair is left out of the solve so the drawing stays as it was.

Constraints that refer to geometry the solver does not handle (an ellipse, a polyline vertex that does not exist) are left out and listed in the message.

Driving dimensions

A dimension with a driving value adds a constraint on the geometry it measures. A dimension stores positions, not references, so its points are matched to entity points by coordinates: the first entity point (in drawing order) within 1e-9 mm of the dimension's point. When a point matches nothing, the dimension is not applied and the message says so.

Measure-only (non-driving) dimensions do not go to the solver at all: planegcs' non-driving constraints add an unknown of their own, which spoils the degree-of-freedom count, and its own documentation lists several that do not work.

Groups and size

The solver works on one group of connected geometry at a time: entities linked by constraints or driving dimensions. Entities with no constraint are not sent at all (they are free, with the degrees of freedom in the table above). This keeps each solve small: planegcs analyses a system with a dense matrix in a fixed memory of about 21 MB, so one system with thousands of unrelated entities would be slow and soon run out of memory, while many small ones are fast (a chain of 100 joined lines solves in well under 100 ms on a laptop).

One group may not exceed about 200,000 (unknowns × constraints): roughly a chain of 150 joined lines with a horizontal or vertical constraint each. A larger group is not solved; the message says so and suggests splitting it. Should planegcs still run out of memory, the worker starts a new copy of the engine for the next request.

What the message says

Every solve returns a diagnosis: solved, conflicting, redundant, failed or empty, the degrees of freedom left, the constraint ids involved, and a sentence for the command line, in English or Italian, for example:

  • "Solved; fully constrained."
  • "Solved. The sketch has 3 degrees of freedom left (for example line e7 can still move)."
  • "Over-constrained: the horizontal constraint on line l12 conflicts with the angle 30° between line l12 and line l7. Remove one of them."
  • "Solved, but the horizontal constraint on line a is redundant: the other constraints already fix the same thing. You can delete it."

Entities are named by type and a short id (sequential ids as they are, random ids by their first four characters); the UI uses the ids in the diagnosis to highlight them on the drawing.

failed means planegcs found no solution without being able to point at the constraints: typically constraints that contradict each other only for the actual shape (an angle of 30° between two lines that are both horizontal), or geometry too far from the intended shape. On failed the group's entities are left unchanged. On conflicting the solver's best attempt is returned with the diagnosis.

Dragging

While the user drags a point, the solver adds a temporary constraint pulling that point towards the cursor. Temporary constraints give way to the real ones (planegcs switches to its SQP method), so the point follows the cursor as far as the sketch allows: dragging a corner of a 100 × 60 rectangle moves the whole rectangle, still 100 × 60.

Degree-of-freedom colouring

freedom tells, per entity, whether the solver can still move it (free), cannot (constrained), or does not know (unknown). Method:

  1. Entities with no constraint are free; polylines with arcs are fixed.
  2. Each group is solved. If it is over-constrained or the solve fails, its entities are unknown.
  3. Every point of the group is pulled 1 mm in a different direction (golden-angle steps, so no two pulls line up) and every radius 1 mm larger, all with temporary constraints, and the group is solved again. The real constraints keep what they determine exactly in place; whatever can still move does. An entity is free when any of its points (or its radius) moved more than 1e-7 mm.

That is two solves per group instead of one per entity.

Limits:

  • Above maxEntities solver entities (300 by default) everything is unknown, to keep the colouring cheap while editing.
  • The test looks at small movements: freedom that only exists far from the current shape (a mechanism that jams) is not seen.
  • In theory the pulls could cancel out for a particular degree of freedom; with irrational pull directions this does not happen in practice.
